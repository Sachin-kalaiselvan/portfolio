    Command history

    Training data

    Every goal the arm works on is recorded as an episode: the instruction, the goal it was turned into, the scene before and after, each pick and place with the joint angles at grasp and release, slips and knocks, and the joint trajectory 10 times a second. The format suits imitation learning and evaluation sets.

    Language test

    50 instructions labelled by hand with the goal they mean, including 6 the arm should refuse. Each is read against the same fixed scene (blue on red, the rest on the table) and the goal that comes back is compared with the label. Nothing moves.

    Language in, physics out, and a loop that checks its own work

    This is the pattern robotics teams are betting on: a language model for meaning, classical planning and control for precision, and perception closing the loop.

    Understanding the words

    Your sentence goes to Claude together with the current scene. Claude does not move the arm. It calls one tool, set_goal, describing the end state: which block should sit on what, or which blocks go in a row. The page checks the goal (no unknown blocks, no loops, no two blocks on one) and sends errors back so Claude can fix them. Questions about the scene are answered with a get_scene tool instead. When Claude is not available to the viewer, a small built-in parser handles the common commands.

    Planning the moves

    The goal becomes a blocks-world problem. The planner is the classic complete strategy: put a block in its final place whenever its support is already correct, otherwise clear away a block that is in the way. It solves the Sussman anomaly in three moves.

    Moving the arm

    Each move is a pick and a place. Inverse kinematics is solved analytically for a five-joint arm with the gripper pointing down: base yaw, then a two-link solution for shoulder and elbow, the wrist holding the tool vertical, and a roll joint that turns the fingers to match the cube's faces. Approaches are straight vertical lines; transfers sweep at a safe height above the tallest stack.

    Sorting, kitting and training data

    Bins turn the same loop into factory work: a job is a pick list that says which blocks go in which bin, and the arm chooses a free slot in each bin, clears anything in the way and repairs the result if it is disturbed. Every goal is also recorded as an episode, with the joint trajectory, so the runs can be downloaded as training or test data for a learned policy.

    Closing the loop

    The cubes live in a rigid-body physics engine. Grasps can slip (you choose how often), towers can be knocked over, and you can drag blocks yourself. After every place, and every second while it keeps the goal, the arm reads the scene back into symbols and plans again from the truth.
