1 · sort the demand

Every item gets classed by how often it sells (avg gap between sales, ADI) and how jumpy the quantities are (CV²), syntetos / boylan / croston style. Stuff that sells most days is smooth or erratic. Stuff with lots of zero days is intermittent or lumpy.

2 · methods fight it out

Smooth items try seasonal naive, SES, damped trend, holt-winters (additive or multiplicative, AICc picks), theta and the M4 "comb" average. Intermittent ones try croston, croston SBA and TSB. Each one gets refit at 3 earlier dates and scored on the 28 days after. Lowest MASE wins. You can override it.

3 · safety stock from real misses

The winner gets refit at 8 earlier dates and the planner logs how far off its total was over the protection window (lead time + review period). The spread of those actual misses sets the safety stock, not some textbook formula. Cycle service target → normal quantile. Fill rate target → solves the normal loss function so expected shortage per cycle = (1 − target) × order size.

4 · when + how much

Top up every review orders up to S on a fixed day. order when low only fires when stock + what's on the way drops to the reorder point s, and orders an economic quantity. That reorder point also bakes in how far stock usually dips below s before the next check.

5 · test it on your own history

The policy screen replays last year's real sales day by day, deliveries landing after the lead time, so you can see every stock-out it would've had.

What it doesn't do

No promos or price effects, no festival calendar (a diwali spike is just noise unless you've got a few years of it), no multi-warehouse transfers, no supplier capacity limits.
